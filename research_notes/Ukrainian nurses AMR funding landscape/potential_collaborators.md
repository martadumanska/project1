## 1. Ukrainian government and policy bodies, Ukrainian academia, professional associations and researchers

### Takeaway
The single most important new Ukrainian partner is the **State Institution "Centre for Nursing Development of the Ministry of Health of Ukraine"**. Its director, Kateryna Komar, is also described as Chief Nursing Officer. The Centre owns the MoH's Strategic Directions for Nursing Development (Nov 2024), the 2025–2027 nursing Roadmap and the nursing professional standards. It is the natural policy-uptake channel for Workstreams 3–5.

The **Public Health Center of Ukraine (PHC/UPHC)** is the AMR policy and surveillance counterpart. It hosts the AMR unit Norway helped set up, and it partners Folkhälsomyndigheten, NIPH, CDC/ICAP and WHO. It is needed for Workstream 4 and the State Strategy to 2030.

The **National Health Service of Ukraine (NHSU)** oversees hospital IPC plans.

On the academic side, **Ternopil National Medical University (Horbachevsky)** has an accredited PhD programme in Nursing (specialty 223) and publishes the journal *Nursing*. It is the strongest Ukrainian partner for doctoral pathways and curriculum assessment, and it sits in the same city as the existing hospital partner. **Kharkiv NMU** and **Vinnytsia National Pirogov Memorial Medical University** lead Ukrainian war-related AMR/HAI research.

### Inferences
- **Centre for Nursing Development (MoH)** should be a formal partner or co-applicant if MoH rules allow; if not, give it a high-level Letter of Support and a seat on the Advisory Board.
  - Why: it is the national owner of nursing scope-of-practice, standards and the 2025–2027 Roadmap. Soruchan's "redistribution of clinical functions" quote fits Workstream 4's task-shifting analysis.
  - What it adds: maximum Ukrainian ownership and a direct uptake channel for curriculum (WS3) and role (WS4) recommendations.
- **PHC Ukraine (AMR unit / IPC team)** should give a Letter of Support and sit on the Advisory Board. Ideally a named PHC AMR or IPC lead co-authors the WS4 policy synthesis.
  - Why: PHC is the technical body implementing the State Strategy to 2030 and the Operational Plan. Its 2025 PPS/AMR-index data would give WS2 a baseline and comparison.
  - Caution: PHC is already engaged by Norway, Sweden (AMR Contain) and CDC/ICAP. The application should frame itself as complementary, contributing the nursing and education angle.
- **MoH AMR coordination**, the Department of Medical Services or the Deputy Minister responsible, should give a Letter of Support that cites the State Strategy and Operational Plan by name. NHSU fits as a dissemination and policy-uptake channel. Possible lever, to be tested: NHSU contracting requirements for hospital IPC programmes could recognise nurse IPC roles.
- **TNMU** should be a formal academic partner for WS3 (curriculum assessment) and WS5 (doctoral pathways), or at least a co-supervision partner.
  - Why: it is one of the few Ukrainian universities with a running nursing PhD and its own nursing journal (*Nursing*), which can serve as a Ukrainian-language dissemination outlet. It is also in Ternopil, the same city as the existing hospital partner and one of the CDC/ICAP IPC pilot regions.
  - Caveat: it is plausible, but not verified, that Ternopil Regional Clinical Hospital is a TNMU clinical base.
- **KhNMU (Chumachenko group)** and **VNMU (Nazarchuk, Kondratiuk)** fit as scientific advisory members or WS2/WS4 collaborators on the HAI and war-wound AMR evidence base. KhNMU gives an eastern, frontline-region counterweight to Ternopil and Kyiv and also links to Swedish AMR Contain. VNMU links to the Swedish Lund/EUCAST network.
- The **First Lviv Territorial Medical Union** could be a second clinical site or a dissemination partner, given its Charité/RKI links and western-Ukraine referral role.
- **IMBG / SURE-AMR** would add a One Health research-excellence partner for future Horizon Europe or OHAMR bids. Its mobility programme could host nurse researchers. It is a low-cost Letter of Support.
- **Shupyk National Healthcare University** is the national postgraduate and continuing-education university. It already works with KI on young-researcher support, which makes it a natural WS5 partner through the existing KI partner.

## 2. International technical bodies (WHO, ECDC, ICARS, Quadripartite, ICN, EFN, ESNO, ESCMID/EUCIC, IPS, IFIC, GIPCN)

### Takeaway
The verifiable, high-value international bodies are WHO (HQ, WHO/Europe and the WHO Country Office in Ukraine) and the International Council of Nurses.

**WHO** offers three channels:
- WHO HQ authored the AMR/IPC curriculum assessment tool for nursing and midwifery that WS3 will apply.
- WHO/Europe's nursing and midwifery unit is supporting the Centre for Nursing Development on education reform.
- The WHO Country Office runs national IPC schools.

**ICN** already has a direct relationship with Ukrainian nursing leadership through #NursesForPeace and its Humanitarian Fund.

ECDC's role is visible only as a listed partner of a Charité/RKI event. EFN membership of a Ukrainian association, and EUCIC, IFIC, ICARS and GIPCN activity in Ukraine, could not be confirmed.

### Cited Findings

#### WHO HQ: AMR and IPC curriculum assessment tool for nursing and midwifery education
- WHO published the tool (ISBN 9789240122369) for a rapid, systematic review of pre-service nursing and midwifery curricula at institutional or national level. It assesses AMR and IPC content and competences to inform targeted curriculum strengthening. It extends the 2024 *AMR Curriculum Assessment Tool for Medical Education*. — [WHO publication](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240122369); [WHO medical-education tool](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240098220)
- Press coverage says it applies to undergraduate, postgraduate and in-service training. It recommends review workshops with nursing faculty, microbiologists, pharmacists and clinical trainers. — [Devdiscourse](https://www.devdiscourse.com/article/health/3907961-who-launches-global-tool-to-improve-amr-and-infection-control-training-for-nurses)
- WHO also publishes a pre-service IPC curriculum for nursing, medicine, midwifery, dentistry, public health and allied health students. It is mentioned in the WHO IPC newsletter of May 2025. — [WHO IPC newsletter, 30 May 2025](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/integrated-health-services-(ihs)/infection-prevention-and-control/newsletter/ipc_newsletter_30-may-2025_final.pdf)

#### WHO Country Office Ukraine (IPC/AMR)
- From 2023 to 2025, four WHO Country Office IPC schools trained 142 medical professionals from all regions, combining distance learning with a three-day practicum. ## 3. Swedish partners that strengthen Sida and Swedish funding fit

### Takeaway
The decisive Swedish finding is **Folkhälsomyndigheten's "AMR Contain" programme (2025–2027)**. It is a Swedish development-aid-funded support programme for Ukrainian hospitals on vårdhygien/IPC and AMR, partnering PHC Kyiv, Kharkiv NMU and the Kharkiv and Lviv rCDCs. It sits under **Socialstyrelsen's** government mandate to coordinate Swedish health reform cooperation with Ukraine; Socialstyrelsen's Sida-funded programme totals SEK 129.5M for 2025–2027. ReAct's own 2025 Ukraine NAP project is already described as being in collaboration with the Swedish public health agency.

The application should present itself as the nursing, research and education complement to AMR Contain, with Folkhälsomyndigheten in a formal advisory or policy role, and continuity beyond AMR Contain's December 2027 end. The other strong Swedish science link is **Lund University (Riesbeck) and the EUCAST Development Laboratory in Växjö (Kahlmeter)**, with KI co-authorship, on Ukrainian war-wound AMR.

## 5. Fit with Sida priorities (Ukrainian ownership, gender equality, poverty/vulnerability, policy uptake) and with alternative funders (EU OHAMR, Horizon Europe, Elrha R2HC, Wellcome)

### Takeaway
For Sida, four additions matter most:
1. Folkhälsomyndigheten (AMR Contain) and Socialstyrelsen, for coherence with Sida-funded Swedish health cooperation with Ukraine.
2. The MoH Centre for Nursing Development and PHC Ukraine, for Ukrainian ownership and policy uptake.
3. TNMU, for a Ukrainian-owned doctoral pathway.
4. WHO (Country Office plus WHO/Europe nursing), the ICN and the Ukrainian Red Cross, for legitimacy and reach.

For alternative funders:
- **EU OHAMR**'s first (2026) call was on treatments, which fits a nursing IPC project poorly, and Ukraine's participation is unconfirmed.
- **Horizon Europe Widening** is viable for Ukrainian-led capacity building, as IMBG's SURE-AMR shows.
- **Elrha R2HC**'s current phase runs 2020–2026 and no AMR-specific call was found.
- **Wellcome** was not researched.

**How each Sida priority is covered:**
- **Ukrainian ownership:** the shortlist is weighted towards Ukrainian state bodies (Centre for Nursing Development, PHC, MoH, NHSU), a Ukrainian university that awards doctorates (TNMU) and Ukrainian civil society (URCS, Patients of Ukraine), on top of the existing Ukrainian partners. A Ukrainian co-chair of the Advisory Board, for example from the Centre for Nursing Development or PHC, would make this visible.
- **Gender equality:** WS4's gender and hierarchy analysis gains credibility if the Advisory Board includes the national nursing leadership (a Chief Nursing Officer) and an organisation with a gender or workforce mandate. No gender-specific Ukrainian health-workforce organisation was identified (see Gaps). The WHO/Europe nursing-shortage work on motivation and career pathways is a citable policy hook.
- **Poverty and vulnerability:** URCS, ICRC and the frontline regions (Kharkiv via KhNMU) extend reach to war-affected and displaced populations. UNDP's IDP-centre infectious-disease training shows a vulnerable-setting IPC need.
- **Policy uptake:** the Centre for Nursing Development (nursing standards, roadmap), PHC (State Strategy and Operational Plan; note the Operational Plan ends in 2026, so the project can inform the next plan), NHSU (IPC plan oversight), WHO and Folkhälsomyndigheten.
